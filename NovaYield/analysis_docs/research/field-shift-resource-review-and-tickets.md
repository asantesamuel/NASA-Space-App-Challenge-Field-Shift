# Field Shift resource review and two implementation tickets

14 November 2026 · NovaYield Ghana pilot · repository reviewed: `dev` at `a8c8f61`

## Resource analysis

The [2026 NASA Space Apps Field Shift challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/field-shift-adapting-farms-with-nasa-data/) asks teams to combine Earth observations, local soils, crop characteristics and farmer priorities for rotation decisions. I verified the indexed official objective. Direct retrieval of the full challenge page returned a gateway error, so coverage below follows all 24 entries in the supplied resource list; each entry is assessed against its provider's documentation or an original study. This was a desk and code review: no new NASA granules were downloaded, no GAEZ value was sampled at the pilot points, and no live MongoDB state was inspected.

### Earth observations, global models and partner resources

| Resource and method | What it can answer for NovaYield, and disposition |
| --- | --- |
| **NASA POWER** — processed daily agroclimate data at source-native resolution; meteorological inputs include MERRA-2 and GEOS-IT. MERRA-2 land precipitation is observationally corrected. The documented MERRA-2 meteorological grid is about 0.5° × 0.625°. [Daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) · [Meteorological sources](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources_meteorology/) | **Keep as primary.** The long daily archive can support matched-date totals, heat and dry-spell comparisons. The three approved parameters do not measure field rain, root-zone water or crop water use. POWER's broader parameter catalog is not a reason to add variables. |
| **GPM / IMERG** — passive-microwave and infrared estimates blended on a 0.1° grid; Final adds monthly gauge adjustment and arrives with a multi-month lag. GES DISC publishes numeric daily data and supports subsetting; [Worldview](https://software.nasa.gov/software/GSC-17111-1) is useful to inspect events, but a rendered image does not add measurement detail or provide an analysis series. [Daily Final V07 catalog](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/gpm-imerg-final-precipitation-l3-1-day-0-1-degree-x-0-1-degree-v07-gpm_3imergdf) · [NASA data directory](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/directory) · [Version transition](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/update-imerg-v08-transition-schedule-aug-2026) | **Use for an offline cross-check.** Pair identical UTC dates at the existing town references. This compares a finer local grid estimate with POWER's broader one; agreement does not establish either as correct. A [2026 Volta Basin gauge study](https://journals.ametsoc.org/abstract/journals/hydr/27/8/JHM-D-25-0104.1.xml) found substantially weaker daily agreement than monthly agreement; [a southern Ghana process study](https://doi.org/10.1175/JHM-D-19-0257.1) found errors that vary by rain type and retrieval source in V06B. Neither establishes accuracy for these three towns. Pin Final V07/V07B and use a historical interval before its September 2025 endpoint; do not splice Early/Late or infer V08 availability from transition announcements. |
| **SMAP** — `SPL3SMP_E` v6 reports quality-flagged radiometer estimates on a daily 9 km posted grid from 2015; surface retrieval refers to roughly the top 5 cm and the underlying footprint is broader than the grid spacing. Root-zone L4 is a separate modeled analysis. [NSIDC product](https://nsidc.org/data/spl3smp_e/versions/6) · [Earthdata Harmony access](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/data-announcements/user-notice-nasa-earthdata-harmony-data-transformation-services-now-available-smap-l2-l4-data-sets) | **Defer.** Useful if the team later needs a regional surface-wetting or drought comparison. The snapshot can miss rain-to-drying transitions, and flags, overpass timing, vegetation and mixed land surface matter. It cannot estimate plant-available water following harvest or verify water stress on a farmer's plot. Freeze/thaw adds little to this Ghana use case. |
| **Landsat / MODIS / VIIRS** — Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 supplies 30 m corrected reflectance and quality bands. MODIS MOD13Q1 (250 m) and VIIRS VNP13A1 (500 m) supply 16-day vegetation-index composites with quality flags. [USGS Landsat Level-2](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-level-2-science-products) · [Landsat QA](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-quality-assessment-bands) · [MODIS product](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD13Q1.061) · [NASA VIIRS product](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VNP13A1) | **Defer.** Landsat can show cover trajectories inside verified field boundaries; MODIS/VIIRS can show district greenness, with mixed pixels and a composite observation date that need not equal the labeled period start. Use cloud/shadow masking and comparable seasons. Greenness alone cannot identify crop, management, or stress cause. Town coordinates and dated self-reports are not imagery training labels. |
| **SRTM** — February 2000 radar-derived elevation, with a global ~30 m product over the mission footprint. Slope and flow direction are derivatives of the DEM. [NASA product guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-05/SRTM_Quick_Guide.pdf) | **Defer.** A terrain layer could flag slopes for follow-up if verified plot boundaries and soil/management evidence become available. Radar surface, vegetation, void filling, pixel alignment, roads and contouring limit local interpretation. A slope at the town point cannot diagnose a farmer's erosion or infiltration. |
| **ECOSTRESS** — ISS thermal observations; L2 records land-surface skin temperature, and L3 `ECO_L3T_JET` estimates ET from models and inputs at nominal 70 m. Clouds, overpass time and irregular sampling matter. [JPL product catalog](https://ecostress.jpl.nasa.gov/data) · [NASA collection directory](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/site/collections/directory/LPCLOUD/gov.nasa.eosdis) | **Defer.** Matching verified field boundaries and clear overpasses could contextualize unusual hot surfaces or ET, but skin temperature differs from POWER air temperature. ET is neither irrigation applied nor crop demand by itself. No reliable daily stress or shallow-root diagnosis from this MVP's location input. |
| **NLDAS / GLDAS** — model-generated land states and fluxes from meteorological forcing; NLDAS is hourly at 1/8° over central North America, while one GLDAS option is global Noah v2.1 at 0.25° and three-hourly. [NASA NLDAS](https://ldas.gsfc.nasa.gov/nldas) · [GLDAS documentation](https://ldas.gsfc.nasa.gov/gldas) · [Noah collection](https://doi.org/10.5067/E7TYRXPJKWOQ) | **Exclude NLDAS for geography; defer GLDAS.** GLDAS could compare regional model-derived moisture/fluxes with SMAP or gauge evidence later. Layer depths, forcing overlap and changing versions complicate interpretation, and modeled root-zone water is not a local reading. Extra subdaily data do not address the calendar gap. |
| **FAO / IIASA GAEZ** — v5 combines AgERA5 historical climate, crop-specific land utilization types, soil constraints (HWSD v2.2), terrain and assumptions about water supply and inputs. Key suitability outputs are published at ~1 km; use the actual selected layer's scale. [FAO v5 overview](https://www.fao.org/gaez/en) · [Model: climate and soil integration](https://github.com/un-fao/gaezv5/wiki/07.-Module-V-(Integration-of-climatic-and-edaphic-evaluation)) · [Data portal](https://data.apps.fao.org/gaez/?lang=en) | **Use for evidence review.** Compare historical rain-fed suitability for candidate crops where the crop/species and management scenario match. The output can flag a question for local review. It does not establish planting dates, labour, plot soil condition, realised yields or a rice system. GAEZ's climatology is separate from the POWER normal; do not add its suitability to the existing soil/water scores. |
| **GEDI** — ISS waveform lidar measures sampled canopy structure; L4A estimates aboveground biomass at ~25 m footprints and L4B aggregates to ~1 km with uncertainty. Coverage is sampled along tracks rather than continuous plot imagery. [GEDI products](https://gedi.umd.edu/dataproducts/products/) | **Defer.** Useful for a separate agroforestry or surrounding woody-cover study if adequate footprints exist. Footprint biomass is not crop stress, crop biomass, soil health, or a rotation outcome. |
| **ESA Sentinel-2** — Level-2A surface reflectance, 10 m red/NIR and 20 m red-edge/SWIR bands; nominal five-day two-satellite revisit does not mean five-day clear imagery. Browse with Copernicus Data Space, STAC or its processing services. [Copernicus Sentinel-2](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/data-collections/copernicus-sentinel-missions/sentinel-2) | **Defer.** A stronger candidate for small-field cover monitoring than coarse composites once verified plot geometry and a clear use case exist. Check cloud/shadow, valid pixel counts and minimum field size. NDVI/red-edge responses do not distinguish drought from harvest, pests or nutrient limits by themselves. |
| **ECMWF ERA5** — global hourly reanalysis, available since 1940; the CDS single-level atmospheric product is served at 0.25°. Data assimilation fills gaps with a model informed by observations. [Climate Data Store](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-single-levels?tab=overview) | **Defer.** It is a candidate for a documented temperature sensitivity study; match UTC windows and handle accumulated variables and units correctly. It is not independent field truth and should not be used to fill POWER nulls or silently extend/replace its normals. Rainfall comparison has higher direct relevance and lower scope for this pass. |

### Surveys, observations, imagery and statistics with limited transfer to Ghana

| Resource and method | What transfers, and what does not |
| --- | --- |
| **USDA Ag Data Commons** — research repository, not one harmonized soil or crop API. A deposit has its own experiment, locations, license, methods and data dictionary. [Repository](https://agdatacommons.nal.usda.gov/) | **No integration.** Its dataset-level citation and evidence model is useful. No directly transferable Kintampo rotation or plot dataset was established in this review. A U.S. trial can inform a documented analogue only when crop, management, climate and response are comparable. |
| **NRCS Web Soil Survey (WSS)** — official U.S. area-of-interest map and interpretation interface built around published survey data. [WSS guide](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/conservation-basics/soil/getting-started-with-web-soil-survey) | **Exclude for geography.** Its separation of map unit, interpretation and limitations is a good example for reading regional models. A survey map unit can include multiple soil components; even in coverage it does not replace a field sample. |
| **NRCS SSURGO / STATSGO2** — SSURGO links detailed survey polygons to soil components, horizons and interpretations; STATSGO2 generalizes soil associations for regional planning, usually at 1:250,000 in the continental U.S. [SSURGO](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/data-and-reports/soil-survey-geographic-database-ssurgo) · [STATSGO2](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/data-and-reports/description-of-statsgo2-database) | **Exclude for geography.** Neither supplies Ghana texture, drainage, organic matter or water capacity. Borrow the distinction between mapped and sampled properties; do not transfer U.S. soil attribute values. WSS and these products are related access/data layers, not independent confirmations. |
| **USDA Cropland Data Layer / CropScape** — annual supervised crop classification for the U.S.; historical 30 m layers, with 10 m releases beginning in 2024. [NASS release notes](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Research_and_Science/Cropland/Release/index.php) | **Exclude for geography.** Consecutive labels can estimate annual transitions with suitability and classification uncertainty in the U.S. They cannot establish Ghana crop identity, within-year planting/harvest dates, double-cropping history, or rice ecology. |
| **OSU PRISM** — U.S. gridded station-based climate interpolated using terrain relationships; daily grids and 1991–2020 normals have product-specific resolutions. [PRISM data](https://www.prism.oregonstate.edu/data/) · [Normals](https://www.prism.oregonstate.edu/normals/) | **Exclude for geography.** It is a useful benchmark pattern: assess gridded data against local observations in the same time window. Its maps and point estimates provide no Ghana baseline, and station interpolation is still a model estimate. |
| **USDA NAIP** — growing-season U.S. aerial orthophotography with metre or sub-metre products depending on acquisition; generally a multi-year collection cycle. [USGS imagery acquisition notes](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-often-orthoimagery-national-map-updated-and-what-are-acquisition-dates) | **Exclude for geography.** High-resolution imagery is useful to verify boundary geometry where available, but an acquisition snapshot cannot establish a dated rotation or its field performance. |
| **USGS NLCD** — 30 m U.S. broad land-cover classes and change products; Annual NLCD covers the contiguous U.S. [USGS annual NLCD](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/annual-national-land-cover-database) | **Exclude for geography.** It could stratify landscape context in a U.S. deployment. A land-cover class is neither a crop sequence nor direct evidence of pest pressure, microclimate, or runoff. |
| **CoCoRaHS** — volunteer gauge precipitation across the U.S., Canada and participating territories/islands. Daily reports have an observation time and may be missing, trace, or multi-day. [Network](https://www.cocorahs.org/?idioma=eng) · [Map and observation times](https://maphub.cocorahs.org/) | **Use the method, not the dataset.** There is no Ghana pilot network in this resource. Adopt the principle of comparing same-period gauge totals against gridded estimates when appropriate data rights, quality flags and compatible reporting intervals exist. [GMet data request](https://www.meteo.gov.gh/data-request/) is a Ghana route; no data access is assumed or requested here. |
| **USGS NWIS** — monitoring-location series for streamflow, groundwater, stage and water quality, now exposed via Water Data for the Nation APIs. [USGS API migration](https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/docs/ogcapi/migration/) | **Exclude for geography.** Even a nearby gauge measures conditions at its monitoring location. It does not establish farm access, legal allocation, storage, water quality at a plot, or irrigation reliability. |
| **USDA AMS Market News** — report-specific market surveys, prices and volumes via MyMarketNews and its API. [AMS reports](https://mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov/) · [API documentation](https://mymarketnews.ams.usda.gov/mymarketnews-api) | **Exclude from scoring.** A reported market price depends on location, channel, grade, date and units. These reports do not establish Ghana farmgate prices, buyer access or margins; useful questions for future local discovery, not a justified fifth criterion. |
| **USDA NASS Quick Stats** — queryable U.S. survey/census estimates by crop, time and geography, including county/state yields and acreage. [NASS query tool](https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov/) | **Exclude for geography.** County averages can benchmark plausibility in a suitable U.S. project. They are not observations of a plot's previous crop, its yield response to rotation, or Ghana crop evidence. |
| **OpenET** — ~30 m ET ensemble of satellite-driven models; the method and validation extend beyond the original western-U.S. framing to the contiguous U.S. The comparison against flux towers is validation of model estimates; OpenET outputs are not themselves measurements. [OpenET validation study](https://etdata.org/literature/assessing-the-accuracy-of-openet-satellite-based-evapotranspiration-data-to-support-water-resource-and-land-management-applications/) | **Exclude for this pilot.** Validation against local observations and reporting model spread are useful patterns. U.S. estimates do not demonstrate Ghana water use, or by themselves quantify irrigation or future crop-water requirements. |
| **USFS LANDFIRE** — U.S. modeled vegetation type, cover, height and fuels products by extent, with versioned maps and service access. [Vegetation products](https://www.landfire.gov/vegetation) · [Extents](https://landfire.gov/data) | **Exclude for geography and purpose.** Landscape type and perennial cover can be useful in a separate agroforestry project. LANDFIRE does not supply verified Ghana field boundaries, crop history or crop-rotation traits. |

## Prioritized improvements

1. **Make Kintampo's existing location usable with sourced provisional calendars.** Kintampo's missing dates are a direct blocker to the existing recommendation workflow. Ghana MoFA's [maize guide, §5.1](https://mofa.gov.gh/site/images/pdf/production_guides/GUIDE_TO_MAIZE_PRODUCTION.pdf) gives transition-zone maize windows, and a [Kintampo North yam trial, Table 1](https://integrityresjournals.org/journal/JASP/article-abstract/CDDF73595) provides actual Dente white-yam planting dates: 21 June in 2015 and 9–10 June in 2016 at the four communities. The [municipal vulnerability assessment, §3.1.4](https://napglobalnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ghana-2024-cvra-report-kintampo.pdf) reports shifting rainy seasons and community variation. That evidence supports carefully labeled starter windows, not a local forecast or certification. Reuse the canonical catalog, operator seeds and Comparison UI; show evidence links, scope and caveats by scheduled step, including in saved comparisons. Use GAEZ to review whether the regional crop/management assumptions raise questions, not to create dates. **Benefit:** useful options for eligible Kintampo farms and a visible explanation of what the schedule assumes. **Effort:** medium, mostly catalog provenance, a small additive output change and regression tests; local confidence remains limited.
2. **Measure how much the rainfall signal depends on its source before adding a new production dependency.** Use IMERG Final daily data as an operator-run comparison against stored POWER, with paired UTC dates, identical dry thresholds and explicit gaps. Deliver a reviewable report by pilot town and window. Differences can reflect grid footprint, estimate method and timing; the report should expose disagreement, not automatically correct POWER or adjust recommendations. The CoCoRaHS pattern gives a clear next standard of evidence: held-out local gauges with comparable measurement periods, if the team later obtains them. **Benefit:** quantifies uncertainty in a decision input already used by the MVP and makes any future proposal for extra rainfall data defensible. **Effort:** medium, bounded to one input product and an offline report. There is no new service to operate.

Nothing else is proposed for implementation. Optical imagery, temperature/ET, soil moisture, terrain and markets need either field geometry, relevant field observations or a new product decision to change what NovaYield can responsibly say. An operator audit is a better first use of these resources than a second live scoring feed.

## Implementation Ticket 1 — Add provisional Kintampo planting windows and show their evidence in rotation comparisons

**Objective:** Make the existing scheduler capable of producing eligible Kintampo rotations and let farmers see exactly what supports each new planting window, including when reopening a saved comparison.

**Background and context:** The seed has no Kintampo rows. Ghana MoFA confirms [maize and yam are grown in Kintampo North](https://mofa.gov.gh/site/directorates/56-district-directorates/district-brong-ahafo/176-kintampo-north-municipal). The [maize guide §5.1](https://mofa.gov.gh/site/images/pdf/production_guides/GUIDE_TO_MAIZE_PRODUCTION.pdf) specifies planting periods by agroecology. The [Frimpong et al. paper, Table 1](https://integrityresjournals.org/journal/JASP/article-abstract/CDDF73595) reports dates directly observed in the district, and identifies the district as forest–savanna transition. The [municipal report, Table 2](https://napglobalnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ghana-2024-cvra-report-kintampo.pdf) describes a general agricultural calendar with variable and shifting rains; it does not establish crop-specific dates. [FAO GAEZ v5](https://www.fao.org/gaez/en) supplies an additional model-based regional suitability check. The model evidence and the planting evidence answer different questions.

**Scope and implementation guidance**

1. Add three Kintampo-only `CropCalendar` seed entries under a new revision such as `product-direction-kintampo-v1`. Use the existing season keys `major-rainy-season` and `minor-rainy-season`. All three entries have `crosses_year=false` and status `provisional`.

   | Crop and season | Inclusive dates | Evidence and required caveat |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Maize, major | `03-15`–`04-30` | Encoding of the MoFA transition-zone range. The 15th represents “mid-March,” not a published exact day. The municipal evidence of shifting rains weakens the transfer of these dates to any particular year or community. |
   | Maize, minor | `07-15`–`09-10` | Encoding of the MoFA transition-zone range. The 15th and 10th are the existing MVP conventions for “mid-July” and “early September,” not precise source observations. Cite MoFA directly for this location. The district seasonal evidence is variable and not a current onset forecast. |
   | Yam, major | `06-09`–`06-21` | Provisional span of Kintampo North's trial planting dates: 21 June 2015 and 9–10 June 2016. Intervening days are a range encoding, not additional observations. A two-season Dente white-yam trial in four communities is an explicitly limited analogue for the generic `yam` key; it did not establish optimal dates, all-species timing, or today's recurring windows. |

2. Add a small operator-facing evidence matrix in `analysis_docs/research/`: one row per pilot location for each of maize and yam (six total). Prefer GAEZ v5 historical 2001–2020 rain-fed suitability with low inputs, where the exact definition and historical layer are available. Use an official thematic layer and its legend, not a country summary, yield raster, screenshot, irrigated layer or future scenario. Record product and layer ID, source citation, native resolution, climate period, water/input assumptions, crop/LUT mapping, retrieval date, native suitability class and transfer limits. Use a single documented native-cell lookup at the existing town reference inside operator tooling, with no coordinates in the output. If no exact scenario is accessible or a GAEZ crop label does not match the needed crop/LUT (especially generic yam vs a specific species), record the reason and `unavailable` or `not_comparable`; never invent a value, interpolate categorical classes or substitute another scenario silently. Differentiate an unavailable layer from a valid low-suitability class. Record any observed mismatch as a question for extension review, not a new hard filter. This is a reproducible desk note, not a new runtime service or required availability condition.
3. Extend the active calendar adapter and the scheduled-event output only as needed to carry optional, structured, allowlisted public evidence: the existing source title/status/version plus the source URL, window basis (zone guidance or local trial analogue for the new rows), applicability and limitations. Reuse existing `CropCalendar.evidence`/`source_note` where possible; add a backward-compatible optional field if the evidence basis cannot otherwise be represented faithfully. Capture the evidence selected for **that step** in the generated payload. Reuse the existing append-only saved result; no read-time catalog lookup or migration of old rows or results. Keep old fields and handle missing new evidence without stale links or guessed status. Use only public document links without coordinate-bearing query parameters, credentials or internal IDs.
4. In `Comparison.tsx` and `api/recommendations.ts`, extend the existing Calendar evidence column or disclosure. Show a source link, scope and limits when available, and keep dates labeled provisional. For Kintampo, explicitly state that the windows are historical planning assumptions and advise checking current planting guidance with local extension; do not promise a forecast integration. Use the same display for freshly generated and saved comparisons. Retain the existing no-options response and the labour/rice checks, one farmer priority, and score and diversity rules. Keep the existing horizon semantics: planting slots must fit within 24 months of the schedule start and the next step uses latest maturity plus the configured turnover; do not introduce a new final-harvest cutoff in this ticket.

**Acceptance criteria**

- The existing seed path inserts exactly the three intended Kintampo rows into a clean seeded database, with one active row per scope. A rerun inserts no duplicates and overwrites no curated active row. Ejura and Anyinasu seeds and crop trait values are unchanged. No rice, cowpea or groundnut Kintampo calendar is added from broad or indirect evidence.
- At a fixed `today` and `available_from` of 15 March 2027, an otherwise eligible Kintampo farm with complete dated history and low labour produces at least one valid three-step option, and all its steps comply with the active calendar, maturity and turnover constraints. In a separate scheduler fixture, a high-labour input with an eligible yam step can place it inside the June row; a high-labour farm is not required to receive yam in the top three. If the required rows are absent, unsupported or the constraints admit no sequence, the workflow returns `no_options` and its reason. Where only maize is feasible, the result is allowed to repeat maize and the existing diversity explanation remains visible.
- The six-row GAEZ matrix identifies real layers/results or actionable availability/mapping gaps, and states that suitability was not used to create dates, change traits, score, or diagnose field soil. No farm records or coordinates appear in the matrix or any artifact.
- A new generated result and its saved owner-scoped copy show the identical calendar evidence. An older result renders without the optional fields and is not recalculated. A different user's ID and a missing ID retain identical not-found behavior. There is no new required farm input, provider call from a farmer request, or additional geo/soil field in the public contract.

**Testing requirements:** Use `test_product_direction_v1.py` for fixed-date scheduler and scoring regressions; use `test_ny39_catalog_repositories.py` and a disposable Mongo integration fixture to verify seeding, unique active scope and a curated-row collision. Use `test_environmental_workflow_api.py`, `test_rotation_result_api.py` and the authenticated integration flow for live-versus-saved response, old shape, redaction, owner and CSRF behavior. Cover existing compliant and no-options cases. Run backend pytest and Ruff plus frontend `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint` and `npm run build`. Manually check the comparison at narrow width with both a new result and a saved result missing the new fields. Use fixtures in CI; record the official sources checked and any unavailable GAEZ layer in the evidence matrix.

## Implementation Ticket 2 — Audit rainfall and dry-spell agreement between NASA POWER and IMERG for the Ghana pilot

**Objective:** Give operators reproducible evidence of where two regional rainfall estimates agree or disagree on inputs relevant to rotation advice, without changing any live climate data or recommendations.

**Background and context:** POWER is the current production source and the rainfall history can affect the first water weight. [NASA GES DISC IMERG Final Daily](https://doi.org/10.5067/GPM/IMERGDF/DAY/07) offers a finer-grid, satellite-based estimate, adjusted using gauge analysis, and can provide a useful cross-check. Ghana gauge studies show why daily and monthly agreement should not be conflated. CoCoRaHS demonstrates the value of a local gauge comparator where one exists; this pilot does not currently have one. The baseline requires a separate decision before adding more NASA datasets to the product. This ticket proposes only an offline evidence tool and report. Assignment does not approve a new production feed, automated download/backfill or scoring change.
